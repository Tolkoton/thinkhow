# Starting notes — sources checked (desk research, 2026-10-27)

Legend: **opened** — the page was fetched and its content read; **search** — the URL was returned by a web
search with a matching title and snippet, the page itself was not fetched; **failed** — the fetch returned an
error (the error code is given). A book named without a URL was not read; what it is said to contain rests
on the pages listed under it.

## complexity-sensemaking
- Snowden D. J., Boone M. E., "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making", HBR, Nov 2007 —
  https://hbr.org/2007/11/a-leaders-framework-for-decision-making — **opened** (title, authors, date; the body
  is behind a paywall). Supports: the context-first idea, five domains (from the search summary).
- Kurtz C. F., Snowden D. J., "The new dynamics of strategy…", IBM Systems Journal 42(3), 2003 —
  https://research.ibm.com/publications/the-new-dynamics-of-strategy-sense-making-in-a-complex-and-complicated-world
  — **opened** (abstract). Supports: the origin of the framework, and the point that earlier methods are still
  valid within their own limits.
- Wikipedia, "Cynefin framework" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynefin — **search**. Supports: the names of
  the domains.

## consider-opposite
- Lord, Lepper, Preston, "Considering the opposite…", JPSP 47(6), 1984 — https://causeweb.org/cause/node/7638
  — **search**. Supports: considering the opposite beat the instruction to "be fair and unbiased".
- Larrick R. P., "Debiasing", Blackwell Handbook of JDM, 2004 — https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1056892 ,
  https://stafforini.com/works/larrick-2004-debiasing/ — **search**. Supports: consider-the-opposite counted
  among the cognitive debiasing strategies.
- Schwarz N. et al. (a review; file name "setting people straight") —
  https://dornsife.usc.edu/norbert-schwarz/wp-content/uploads/sites/231/2023/11/07_aep_schwarz_et_al_setting-people-straight.pdf
  — **search**. Supports: the backfire caveat (reasons that are hard to generate can strengthen the original
  view), citing Larrick 2004 p. 323. The exact title was not verified.

## first-principles

## five-whys

  complex problems; it is useful as a teaching tool.
- Peerally, Carr, Waring, Dixon-Woods, "The problem with root cause analysis", BMJ Qual Saf, 2017 —

  origin and the internal criticism that it is "too basic".

## Note on what the notes add
The typical situations in the router notes are generic examples, generalised from the sources and from
each card's own when_yes / signals. They are not taken from any study, and no real persons appear. The
"where people get stuck" lines either rest on a named source (cited inline in the note) or restate the
card's own traps in practical form.
